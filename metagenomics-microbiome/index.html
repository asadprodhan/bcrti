Build confidence in microbiome research

A sequencing facility can deliver millions of DNA reads. To interpret them, you need to know what was sampled, how the DNA was prepared, which reads are suitable for analysis, and how the results relate to your research question.

You’ll follow the workflow from sample collection and DNA extraction to sequencing, quality control, taxonomic classification and interpretation. The course focuses on shotgun metagenomics, an approach that sequences DNA fragments from across a sample to provide evidence about the organisms and genes represented in it.

Designed for researchers starting their first microbiome study

This course is suitable for

Higher degree by research students planning a study, early career researchers working with sequencing data, and biologists who want to discuss study design and analysis with collaborators.

Relevant research areas

Soil and agriculture, plant science, ecology, animal science, human health and other fields that study microbial communities.

Prerequisites

No previous microbiome analysis experience is required. Commands and file formats are introduced as you use them. Bring a question from your own research if you have one.

What participants will be able to do

  • Plan a microbiome study. Define a testable question, identify independent biological replicates, plan controls and record sample information.
  • Follow the wet lab workflow. Explain how sample handling, DNA extraction, library preparation and sequencing shape the data you receive.
  • Assess and classify sequencing reads. Interpret FastQC reports, justify preprocessing decisions and read Kraken2 taxonomic assignments.
  • Compare microbial communities. Prepare a taxon abundance table, describe community composition and compare diversity within and between samples.
  • Interpret and communicate findings. Assess antimicrobial resistance (AMR) sequence evidence, choose clear figures and explain what the data support.

Flexible, research-focused learning

Duration

Six weeks

Format

Live online classes via Microsoft Teams, with weekly activities in the BCRTI Interactive Learning Space (BILS).

Live commitment

One two-hour session per week, alongside independent practice.

Learn by doing

Each week has an overview and five topics. Learn answers the questions researchers ask when starting a study. In Explore, annotated examples show how to read the evidence. In Apply, you solve a similar problem. Check gives you five multiple-choice questions per topic to assess your understanding.

Demonstrate your learning

Practise the decisions you’ll face in your own research using guided examples and teaching data. In Week 6, you’ll bring the workflow together and complete a 25-question cumulative assessment. BCRTI issues a Certificate of Completion once the course requirements are met.

Designed to support research progression

Research readiness

Decide which samples, controls and sequencing data you need before committing resources to a study.

Computational confidence

Read analysis outputs, identify unexpected patterns and decide what to check next.

Reproducible research

Record inputs, software, reference databases and analysis decisions so others can follow how you reached your findings.
